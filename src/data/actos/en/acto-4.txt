Text 1

I went west,

and I saw El Chaltén, a town of mountains, glaciers, rivers and nights.

I climbed Aconcagua and discovered the white gold of the snow, and I understood America.

I saw the Salinas Grandes, an endless desert of salt where at night it seems you could walk across the stars.

I came to know the vicuñas and the foxes answering each other's calls.

I saw the Hill of Seven Colours: I swear to you I saw a rainbow set into the stone

In the high moorlands I saw the frailejón, a marvellous plant that drinks from the flying rivers to carry them down to the earth. It grows barely a centimetre a year and gives everything it has.

I saw a lagoon in the middle of the Andes where, so the legend goes, men used to hand their gold to the gods.

And in the Wirikuta desert I saw the miracle of the peyote.

I climbed with all my strength and came to know the giant sequoias, tree. Grandparents of yours, eighty metres tall and three thousand years old. They told me they have bark so thick it withstands fire, and cones that open only with the heat of the blaze.

I saw bears, eagles and squirrels.

I heard sacred songs, which were the songs of other birds.

I heard lakes of frogs, crickets and wolves.

I heard the night in its pure state.

I fell in love, tree.

The west, the black door, is wonderful.

Text 2

But I also,

saw the ice of Chaltén and Aconcagua, that white gold I told you about, retreating year after year: there are no longer any laws to defend those altars.

I saw that same desert of salt bored through by men desperate for the lithium that sleeps beneath it. I saw the salt flats sink, and I saw the water of the villages and of the animals that live there die.

I saw the salt workers go out onto the road to defend their water,

and I saw them beaten.

I saw the flamingos left without the lagoons where they nest. I saw them dying slowly.

I saw the vicuña hunted for its wool.

I saw foxes hanging from the wire fences.

In the moorland I saw the frailejón, the one that drinks from the clouds, rotting where it stood, eaten by larvae and fungus because the temperature changed.

I saw that underneath it they are looking for gold. I saw how they tear out the *water-makers* for money

In Wirikuta, where the sun was born, hands tearing up the peyote to sell it faster than it takes to grow.

And I saw the sequoias, tree, your grandmothers, the ones that withstood fire for three thousand years, burning like torches. In two summers one in every five died.

The fire that once opened their cones now kills them.

I saw atrocious deforestation.

I saw the mistreatment of animals.

The polluted lakes.

The lack of water in the communities.

Pain. Much pain.

I saw the hardest poverty of all: that of hearts that are no longer moved.
