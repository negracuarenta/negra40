Text 1

I went south and I saw,

mountains, rivers and glaciers.

I saw the towering Andean condor climbing spirals of warm air that only it can see.

I saw a thousand-year-old forest of araucarias, and motionless lakes that double the sky.

I saw pumas and guanacos running across the Patagonian desert, and the majestic emperor penguin moving over the ice with the sun on its breast.

I crossed the ocean.

At Victoria Falls I saw a curtain of water so wide that the spray rises like a smoke that thunders.

In the Namib I came to know the oldest desert in the world, with red dunes three hundred metres high. And a beetle that drinks from the fog.

And in the Okavango delta I saw a river that never reaches the sea because it spills into the desert. Thanks to its waters I saw the place with the most life in all of Africa.

I saw lions, snakes, zebras and panthers.

I saw the gold of the tigers that a blind man also saw.

I heard the laughter of hyenas, the crash of elephants and the roar of panthers.

I saw volcanoes, sharks, rays, turtles, endless skies, colours, crystals, life, and more life.

The south, tree, is beautiful.

(…)

Text 2

—What else did you see? What else did you see?

I also saw hundred-year-old forests turned to ash by hands that afterwards came to buy the blackened land. Among the ashes I saw deer and rabbits and birds of every kind suffering.

I saw poisoned condors.

It is maddening, tree.

I saw elephants dying beside waterholes that the heat had turned to poison, and machines looking for oil in the river that feeds the Okavango.

I saw that in twenty-five years almost half the lions on earth were lost.

I saw people who have belonged to this land since before maps existed, pushed further and further from it, while it is sold off in pieces to those who will never live on it.

I saw rivers no longer fit to drink from, nor to bathe in, nor to see yourself in.

I saw how they poison the water, tree; it is unbelievable, but I saw it.

I wish I could lie to you, tree, but all of that is what I saw.

(…)

Text 3

The tree wept for four days and four nights.

Then it said

(tree) —I have hope that in the west things are different. Set out in the autumn, and tell me what you see.

(bird) —Will you teach my fledglings to fly, when I am gone?

The tree accepted. The bird left.
